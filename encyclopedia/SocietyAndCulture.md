## River-Valley Civilization

Nearly all ordinary civilization depends upon Khat’Najat. The valley’s warmth, humidity, agriculture, settlements, and trade contrast sharply with the mountains and desert.

Every settlement owes its existence to water. Control of river access, wells, canals, temples, and transport therefore becomes a form of political power.

Amarna is the largest city and combines royal, priestly, commercial, and criminal authority behind a walled exterior.

## Law, Punishment, and the Mines

The mine workers are described as prisoners more than conventional slaves, but the practical distinction is thin. Laws are strict enough that many people receive mine sentences for months, years, or life.

Some people are also bought, sold, or treated explicitly as property.

Undead taskmasters make the punishment system efficient and inhuman:

- They do not tire.
- They do not negotiate.
- They do not forget infractions.

The Bowels of Anubis extends this philosophy to its extreme by preventing the condemned from escaping punishment through death.

## Social Status and Ancestry

Elves are a downtrodden underclass. They are stereotyped as too scrawny or weak for useful labor and can be dismissed socially even when their actual abilities contradict the prejudice.

The remade-world concepts of Favored, Bound, and Unwritten provide an additional possible social layer:

- Favored people benefit from the world’s order but may be closely watched.
- Bound people experience punishment and oppression.
- Unwritten people live ordinary lives without understanding the larger design.

These categories have not yet been formally mapped onto every ancestry or legal class in Amarna.

## Religion in Daily Life

The gods are not remote abstractions. Avatars can walk among people, proclamations can reorganize government, and patron gods shape professional and personal identity.

Sacred images also have practical religious functions. Divine Eyes placed over homes, courts, temples, and public spaces offer gods places through which they may choose to see and hear. Personal holy symbols allow deliberate communication, while maintained idols provide more stable places for communion. None guarantees that a god is attending at a particular moment.

Temple processions are therefore more than pageantry: carrying an idol allows a deity to visit fields, funerals, festivals, or another temple. People who line a procession route may believe the god is literally looking back at them.

Serving as a willing living vessel is an extraordinary act of devotion because divine power can sustain a host through terrible injuries without erasing those injuries. When the god departs, the vessel may collapse, remain gravely wounded, or die.

The great temple of Ra stands physically at Amarna’s center. Public religious doctrine supports social order, but forbidden claims about Amun circulate through survivors, secret worshipers, and groups such as the Stillwater Covenant.

Anubis’s worship reinforces proper burial and the boundary between life and death. Undead can therefore be accepted as state instruments in one context and condemned as sacred violations in another, creating deliberate cultural tension.

## Crime and Order

There is relatively little casual “surface” crime because cartels control serious criminal activity. This does not mean the city is safe. It means violence, smuggling, extortion, trafficking, and corruption are organized.

Underground groups periodically rise into public spaces and create chaos. Covert religious and political organizations also use methods that resemble crime: staged ambushes, secret tests, disappearances, and manipulated evidence.

## Economy and Resources

Standard wealth includes:

- Gold.
- Silver.
- Copper.
- Gems.
- Valuable crafted goods.

Poorer residents frequently barter or trade directly.

Strategic resources include:

- **Salt:** Precious, necessary, preservative, and heavily mined.
- **Nullium:** Anti-magic ore.
- **Cinderquartz:** Newly appearing magical quartz.
- **Stone, gems, and metals:** Foundations of mountain extraction.
- **Water:** The region’s most fundamental resource, even when not treated as coin.

## Travel and Transportation

Established transportation includes:

- Horses.
- Giant lizards used as mounts.
- Hyena-drawn sleds.
- Elephant caravans maintained by nomadic specialists.
- Rare creature-drawn airships available only at great expense.

Caravan travel carries social importance because routes require water knowledge, animal handling, protection, and relationships with nomadic people.

## Death, Undeath, and Eternal Life

Death is both theological and administrative.

- Anubis represents proper passage and judgment.
- State mines use undead overseers as tireless labor enforcement.
- The Bowels preserves life specifically to prolong punishment.
- Tombs, mummies, and catacombs can be sacred rather than automatically evil.
- Improper undead in an Anubis temple represent cosmic disorder.

A common vampire origin story says former Pharaohs were promised eternal life and received undeath instead. The warning is that divine or magical promises may be fulfilled literally and maliciously.

## Djinn and Desperate Bargains

Djinn are considered unnatural tricksters. People seek them when ordinary law, medicine, religion, or wealth has failed.

The cultural warning is consistent: they give what was asked for, not what was wanted. This makes exact wording, motive, and consent central to any encounter.

The party’s decision to free Ashur-Ka and let him choose whether to help creates an unusual relationship based upon agency rather than coercion.

## Knowledge, Heresy, and Skepticism

Restricted libraries, forbidden scrolls, lost cities, and controlled doctrine make knowledge politically dangerous.

Several competing attitudes coexist:

- Official doctrine should be accepted because gods visibly act.
- Amun’s imprisonment is true but suppressed.
- The gods exist but are morally unworthy.
- The beings called gods are powerful but not truly divine.
- Historical reality was rewritten and ordinary scholarship cannot reveal it cleanly.

Sedric, Sylvester, Altennis, and the party’s larger search all place controlled knowledge at the center of the campaign.
