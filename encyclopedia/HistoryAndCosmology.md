## The Previous Multiverse

Before the current world, the six level-20 heroes of the *Vecna: Eve of Ruin* campaign attempted to prevent Vecna from completing the Ritual of Remaking.

The ritual was designed to unravel the multiverse while preserving Vecna’s consciousness in a magical singularity. Vecna could then reknit reality so that he had never known meaningful defeat, all beings ultimately served him, and opposition was rewritten as torment or irrelevance.

## The Final Battle

The plan should have worked. The rod was complete, the timing precise, and the party initially coordinated well enough to make Vecna falter.

A small failure broke that unity. One misstep led to another. Heroes fell, panic spread, and Vecna’s servants poured into the Cave of Shattered Reflection.

Vecna did not rage. He recognized that the outcome had become certain and turned away from the party in dismissal to complete the ritual.

One by one the heroes died. Sylcaryn was the last survivor and escaped to her bastion, but the refuge could not protect her from the remaking of existence.

## The Unraveling

The dead heroes found themselves as spirits above Neverwinter. They could perceive and communicate with one another without words, but Syl was absent because she remained alive.

They found her in the bastion as reality came apart like woven thread:

- Objects loosened into strands of light.
- Buildings lost solidity and dissolved.
- People unraveled without blood or impact.
- Syl’s hand frayed, followed by her body.
- The city, sky, sun, light, and finally the spirits themselves faded.

The process was not an explosion. It was careful, precise unmaking.

The last sensation was a cold presence watching and knowing: Vecna’s ritual had reached completion.

## The Remade Reality

The current world is not a temporary illusion or visibly broken simulation. It is a fully formed reality with deep history, internal consistency, institutions, cultures, religions, and people who ordinarily believe it has always existed.

Most inhabitants have no memory of the previous multiverse. Some experience unease or emotion without context. The player characters and a few exceptional beings can experience stronger echoes.

Early planning divided society into three broad categories:

- **Favored:** Rewarded and privileged, but closely watched.
- **Bound:** Oppressed or punished former opponents.
- **Unwritten:** The majority, living apparently ordinary lives inside Vecna’s design.

These categories remain useful metaphysical or social concepts, though later regional development does not consistently use the labels in public life.

## Memory and Past Lives

Past-life continuity is deliberately flexible.

- The current PCs are not forced reincarnations of specific old heroes.
- Memories can appear as names, dreams, emotional familiarity, reflexive trust or fear, and images of the Unraveling.
- No D&D class feature, level, spell, item, or statistic automatically carries into Daggerheart.
- The current body and identity remain real rather than a disguise over an old character.

The prologue establishes the pattern through the names “Merik” and “Syl.” Intrepid’s inhabitants share dreams of being torn apart, another direct echo of the prior reality.

## Kas as an Anomaly

Kas persists as someone who appears to remember more than the new world should permit. Early design explains this as a blind spot in Vecna’s remaking: Vecna could not fully erase Kas because their history involved alliance, betrayal, hatred, and a deep emotional bond.

Kas’s bard-like presentation in the prologue may be disguise, adaptation, symbolism, or his actual role in the remade world.

## Ra, Vecna, and the Public Cosmology

Early remade-world planning described Vecna as an openly acknowledged creator god with an idealized avatar: blond hair, purple robes, and gold vestments.

Later campaign development instead makes Amun-Ra the dominant public deity and organizes the central conflict around Ra, Amun, the Pharaoh, and competing priesthoods.

These facts create a deliberate unresolved layer:

- Vecna is the metaphysical architect of the current reality.
- Ra is the dominant public divine ruler in the campaign region.
- It is not established whether Ra is independent, rewritten, empowered by Vecna, a mask of Vecna, a rival anomaly, or a being whose history was built into the new world.

The campaign’s strongest thematic question is therefore not only **“Which god is telling the truth?”** but **“What makes any of them gods?”**

## Regional Historical Crisis

The public myth says Ra killed and consumed Amun. The heresy says he imprisoned Amun in the Bowels of Anubis.

The Pharaoh discovered or accepted the heresy, began worshiping Amun, claimed to be Amun’s reincarnation, and attempted to force the priesthood to acknowledge him.

Ra responded with a direct public proclamation denying the claim and removing the priesthood from the Pharaoh’s authority. This is a recent event and a continuing political fracture.

## Campaign Chronology

1. The old heroes confront Vecna and fail.
2. Vecna completes the Ritual of Remaking.
3. The multiverse unravels and is rebuilt.
4. The current characters awaken in the remade world with partial echoes.
5. The party forms around the mines.
6. The Ashen Vow leads the expedition to the ruined city and dies mysteriously.
7. The party explores the Catacombs of Ashur-Ka.
8. Ashur-Ka is freed and the Cinderheart Seal reaches Neshet.
9. Neshet’s intended contact is discovered missing in the Cinderquartz investigation.
10. The party’s larger direction develops toward the Priesthood of Anubis, the lost temple, the Oil of Anubis, and a descent into the Bowels.

## GM Notes and Notable Development

- The openly Vecna-centered society of the earliest setting draft is not erased, but later material changes how it is expressed. Treat it as hidden cosmology or unresolved integration rather than assuming every citizen openly worships Vecna.
- The Favored, Bound, and Unwritten categories are canon concepts but have not yet been thoroughly mapped onto Amarna’s institutions.
- Reality should feel stable. Use subtle historical inconsistencies and emotional echoes rather than frequent visible “glitches.”
- The old campaign’s ending is titled **The Last Secret**; the new opening is **A Life Remembered**.
