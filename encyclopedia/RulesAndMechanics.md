## Hope and Fear

Previously discussed table notes include:

- Player characters begin with **2 Hope**.
- The GM uses Fear as a resource to spotlight adversaries, activate features, escalate scenes, and respond to rolls with Fear.
- Starting Fear and the exact effects of rests were discussed more than once.

Because the official rules may have changed across playtest and final versions, use the current rulebook wording when a precise amount matters.

## Rests and Pressure

A later rules review identified that player rests can generate Fear for the GM. Short and long rests do not represent consequence-free pauses.

The Quartz Below deliberately intensifies this principle. Resting in the mine should carry fictional and mechanical risk, such as:

- Additional Fear.
- Encounter or hazard checks.
- Countdown advancement.
- Crystal growth or environmental change.
- Enemy regrouping.
- Loss of position or safe routes.

The exact official Fear gained from each rest should be checked against the current Daggerheart rulebook rather than the conversational paraphrase.

## Movement and Grid Play

Movement on a tactical grid was discussed for the group’s table setup. Daggerheart uses abstract ranges—Melee, Very Close, Close, Far, and Very Far—rather than requiring a universal feet-per-square conversion.

When using maps:

- Establish a consistent table convention for each range.
- Preserve the narrative purpose of range bands.
- Avoid treating every diagonal or small positional change as a separate rules dispute.
- Use terrain, barriers, elevation, and zones to make movement meaningful.

## Damage and Adversary Handling

Daggerheart damage is compared against character thresholds to determine Minor, Major, or Severe damage and the number of Hit Points marked.

Adversaries use their own thresholds, HP, Stress, attacks, roles, motives, and features. Boss-like humanoids should be built as adversaries rather than full player characters unless a specific rule requires otherwise.

## Conditions and Custom Effects

### Flash Blindness

A developed hazard uses a bright magical flash and a **Difficulty 18 Finesse Reaction Roll**.

Possible results discussed include temporary blindness or a similar impaired state. Duration and removal should be clearly stated in the encounter, for example:

- Until the target’s next action.
- Until the next roll with Hope.
- Until a successful Instinct or Finesse action clears it.
- Until an ally assists.

The exact duration was not permanently standardized across the campaign.

### Vulnerable, Restrained, Hidden, and Other Conditions

Custom adversaries should use official Daggerheart conditions whenever possible rather than creating near-duplicates. Environmental descriptions can remain vivid while the mechanical effect stays concise.

## Divine Possession

Divine possession is a campaign-specific narrative framework rather than a general official Daggerheart subsystem.

- A deity requires a willing, devout host to establish a living-vessel connection.
- The host remains conscious even when the deity assumes full control.
- If the host later tries to expel a deity that refuses to leave, resolve the uncertain struggle with an appropriate **Presence or Instinct Reaction Roll**. Presence suits force of personality or spiritual conviction; Instinct suits self-preservation or an intuitive rejection of the presence.
- A deity acting through a vessel can manifest supernatural abilities and temporary equipment, but the mortal body limits the power it can channel.
- Divine vitality may keep a catastrophically injured host active. When possession ends, existing injuries remain and may immediately incapacitate or kill the host.
- Defeating a possessed champion ordinarily breaks the deity’s material connection rather than killing the deity itself.

The GM should set difficulty and consequences according to the deity, the host’s relationship with it, the degree of surrendered control, and the fictional circumstances. A benevolent patron may leave without requiring a roll.

See [Divine Presence](DivinePresence.md#the-living-vessel) for the full canon.

## Adversary Design

Custom adversaries were created to align with the official presentation. A complete entry ordinarily includes:

- Tier and role.
- One-sentence description.
- Motives and tactics.
- Difficulty.
- Damage thresholds.
- HP and Stress.
- Attack modifier, range, damage, and type.
- Experiences when useful.
- Passive, action, and reaction features.
- Fear costs, Stress costs, countdowns, or spotlight rules.

Campaign-developed examples include Duneveil Stalkers, Corpsebearer Shades, Ash-Salt Dead, Tomb Scorpions, Demon Dogs, Hound Masters, Flame Guardians, Temple Skeletons, and Cinderquartz shard creatures.

## Narrative Carryover from D&D

The previous D&D heroes provide story continuity only.

- No automatic level, spell, class, item, feat, or ability transfer.
- Past-life memories are narrative permissions rather than mechanical bonuses.
- A player may build a completely different Daggerheart character.
- Recognition and dreams should create choices and mysteries, not invalidate the current identity.

## GM Verification Notes

Before a session involving precise mechanics, verify:

- Current party level.
- Current final-rule Hope and Fear values.
- Rest-generated Fear.
- Exact range-to-grid convention.
- Current wording of relevant conditions.
- Whether a custom adversary was built against playtest or final adversary math.
