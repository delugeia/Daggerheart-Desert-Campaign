These houses have regional connections. Vael and Koss are closest to the party. The full extent of wider lineages is not mapped. Vampire house membership is primarily sire-and-progeny lineage, while a household can include mortal retainers and specialists.

How the houses view one another

Institutional views are directional. Private friendship, romance, and commercial exchange can survive contempt or rivalry.

Pair Views and working relationship
Vael ↔ Koss Existing enemies. The party’s attack reinforces the conflict, although Koss has not been shown to have proven Vael’s sponsorship. Corvin is accused of spending Koss money to join Vael, adding a personal ambition within that rivalry; Vael’s response is not specified.
Vael ↔ Morcant Vael: friendly or tentatively allied. Morcant: more neutral and less impressed by Vael’s status, knowing origins and history that Vael has forgotten or inflated. This gives Morcant potential leverage.
Vael ↔ Harrow Vael: common-born upstarts, useful but socially inferior. Harrow: crusty, blue-blooded aristocrats. Vael has relatively little direct need for Harrow’s merchant services, so their contact is principally political.
Vael ↔ Drazek Vael: uncouth and untamed, but powerful enough to fear. Drazek: considers Vael pretentious and overprivileged. This is mutual disdain rather than a specified ongoing war.
Vael ↔ Belrath The houses can cooperate; Seraphine and Lenora personally dislike and try to outdo one another. Edmund and Quill provide an effective personal bridge.
Koss ↔ Morcant They are not especially fond of one another, but exchange knowledge and force. Morcant: finds Koss’s violence crude or messy and can hire or steer it. Koss: obtains useful intelligence; Corvin is the strategist buying Morcant information to prove himself.
Koss ↔ Harrow Koss: dismissive and annoyed by Harrow’s tinkering, mortal ties, and refusal to rise to bullying. Harrow: pities Koss as warriors who do not know better. Business remains possible despite little mutual respect. Corvin and Peregrine’s proposed private relationship provides an individual connection across that divide.
Koss ↔ Drazek Koss: sees barbarians lacking proper discipline. Drazek: sees rigid, rank-obsessed soldiers willing to commit immoral violence for payment. They have fought; Drazek’s home terrain strongly favors its defenders.
Koss ↔ Belrath Belrath: sees Koss as brutes and has little personal respect for it. Belrath can still buy military services from Koss. Paid cooperation remains possible; the strength of reciprocal hostility is unresolved.
Morcant ↔ Harrow They cooperate. Harrow: respects preservation of knowledge over destruction. Morcant: values traveling merchants who bring information, old books, and other discoveries. Harrow need not personally prize a book to recognize its value to Morcant.
Morcant ↔ Drazek Mutual respect built around a shared secret. Morcant: knows Drazek’s custodial duty, supports it with information, and may entrust dangerous finds to it. Differences in manners persist. Ysabet and Othmar’s affair is a separate, private connection.
Morcant ↔ Belrath Generally friendly and willing to exchange knowledge. Both recognize its value, and Belrath is credited with reliably paying its debts. Their different approaches to collecting and using knowledge do not prevent cooperation.
Harrow ↔ Drazek Harrow: likes the guardians’ lack of aristocratic pretension and has more contact with them than many outsiders do. Drazek: finds Harrow useful and broadly acceptable, though perhaps too involved with humans. Mutual respect supports the relationship.
Harrow ↔ Belrath Strong practical complementarity. Belrath: relies on Harrow to sell discoveries and reach mortal buyers. Harrow: trades artifacts and facilitates financial or informational exchange. Belrath finds things; Harrow connects them to a market.
Drazek ↔ Belrath A fundamental conflict over dangerous discoveries. Drazek: keeps dangerous objects from circulation, while secretly awaiting an artifact to restore its elder god. Belrath: recovers, studies, circulates, or sells discoveries. Faris’s information reaches Vera, who will ask the party to give Drazek an expedition find rather than Belrath. This competing claim is specified; theft of existing Drazek holdings remains only a suggestion.

Prior events and optional follow-up

Seraphine's original assignment was a covert strike on Koss's minions, with assistance offered in return and her sponsorship kept secret. The party reports killing subordinate Koss vampires, not destroying the house. It did not announce Vael's involvement. The exact promised assistance remains unspecified, and proof that Koss knows the sponsor is not established.

An optional Black Ledger investigation could follow the raid: it records captive purchases, intermediaries, and payments for attacks on Vael caravans. Hester's duplicates, a compromised Vael agent, and Grim's receipt can complicate its recovery if used. These are optional future complications; the party’s raid is complete.

Shared history and political arrangements

Accounts of the Cistern Siege describe how Koss seized water approaches, Vael sheltered displaced households on conditional terms, Harrow housed survivors, and Drazek evacuated travelers through hidden routes. Belrath recovered damaged archives afterward. Balthazar convened negotiations, with Seraphine helping negotiate the settlement.

The Compact survives as a voluntary political agreement discouraging attacks on acknowledged dependents and providing witnessed dispute proceedings. Morcant preserves records; it is not the universal law-enforcement authority. Coalitions, reputation, bargaining, and force determine what happens when a house violates a commitment.

An optional concealed-annex discovery could supply evidence that certain mortal communities received collective protection. Such evidence could discredit Koss and compromise Morcant's credibility; it does not create an automatic court sentence. Optional witness and ledger evidence should have distinct limits: seeing a transfer, tracing a payment, and proving a leader's order are different claims.
