Character, holdings, and sigil

Koss is a disciplined military house selling organized violence. Rank, formation, predictable punishment, and command matter more than the employer's moral standing. Armed escorts, fortified storehouses, tolls, and collection agents support this power. Its maxim is “Order must be maintained.” Its diamond sigil shows a black fist gripping a crimson chain.

Hester Pike is the true leader. Lord Malrec Koss is the public-facing lord. Outsiders bargain with Malrec and may target him without understanding who governs the household. His imposing portrait remains an accurate depiction of its public authority.

Redstone Bastion, a former caravan fortress overlooking a dry ravine, receives merchants above barracks, cells, and a covered loading yard. Unmarked wagons and competing officers make its administrative workings as important as its walls.

Koss Members

Lord Malrec Koss — public lord

Formal, domineering, and attentive to practical detail, Malrec presents the house's military confidence to the world. He remembers supply prices as readily as insults. He benefits from Hester's direction and provides a visible figure for rivals to confront. His authority is real in public dealings, but it is not the household's final source of power. A possible intimate relationship with Hester remains unresolved.

Malrec is broad-shouldered and square-jawed, appearing in his fifties, with light tawny skin, close-cropped iron-gray hair, and a clean-shaven, severe face. Blackened riveted armor and dark crimson cloth create a heavy silhouette; his formal posture suggests habitual command.

Hester Pike — true leader, outwardly the steward

An exacting administrator whose apparent bookkeeping role hides strategic control. Malrec's prominence allows her to direct money and decisions with less personal exposure. If the Black Ledger is used, she understands its value as leverage and keeps records deliberately. Hester’s nature and the household’s species breakdown remain unspecified. Who besides Malrec knows her authority remains open.

Hester is a slight, tired-looking woman appearing in her fifties, with a warm brown complexion and gray-streaked hair gathered into a tight bun. Plain ochre layers and ink-marked hands suit her work; she often holds a closed black ledger close to her body.

Captain Branka Koss — military commander

Branka directs the soldiers and values discipline, reliable supplies, and useful subordinates. She objects to wasteful cruelty: provoking revolt can be bad command even when she accepts harsh methods. She wants clear authority over forces divided among competing favorites. Her precise knowledge of Hester's role is not decided. She can oppose a disastrous order without becoming a principled opponent of Koss itself.

Branka is a powerfully built adult woman with brown skin, cropped black hair, and an old healed scar along one cheek. Worn dark armor, a crimson officer’s sash, and squared shoulders distinguish her disciplined military bearing from the household’s cultivated elegance.

Corvin Koss — strategist with divided ambitions

Cultivated, impatient, and eager to demonstrate his worth, Corvin buys Morcant information to gain an advantage. He is accused of using Koss house funds to acquire a title and join Vael. This creates a genuine internal conflict without establishing that the accusation is proven or that a transfer is possible. His private relationship with Master Peregrine Harrow expands into a proposed circle including Vera Drazek and Faris Reed. His taste for artists and merchants supplies useful social contacts; his status as a possible heir remains unconfirmed.

Corvin is a slim man appearing around thirty, with pale tawny skin, wavy dark hair falling to his jaw, and an elegantly angular face. His wine-colored robe has restrained metallic embroidery; a lifted chin and carefully arranged hands suggest practiced refinement.

Apparent ages do not establish the vampires' chronological ages.

Historical background

During the Cistern Siege, Koss troops seized approaches to remaining water stores. Malrec calls this the imposition of order; displaced households remember paying to reach water they had maintained. The resulting Compact restrains attacks on acknowledged dependents through political agreement. Its records can support accusations, but Morcant cannot simply command Koss to obey.

The house depends on trade continuing to use its roads. A boycott, damaged logistics, or exposed account books can hurt it alongside battlefield losses. The party's recent strike killed subordinate vampires; it did not destroy the house.

Attitude toward mortals

Koss offers security and predictable rules in exchange for obedience, with rules heavily favoring the house. The house supports servants while exploiting debt laborers and trafficking captives. It can pay contracts reliably while committing brutal acts for the employer. Drazek objects to that moral indifference; Harrow pities the violence and declines to rise to its bullying.

Secrets and internal tensions

  • Hidden leadership: Hester directs the household behind Malrec's public position.
  • Corvin's spending: His alleged diversion of funds and wish to enter Vael threaten discipline and prestige. His Morcant purchases are a separate, identified activity, not proof of the financial accusation.
  • Private connections: Corvin, Peregrine, Vera, and Faris form the proposed intimate circle. Its existence need not mean shared political objectives or unrestricted access to secrets.
  • Optional Black Ledger hook: Records can connect Koss agents to captive purchases and payments for attacks on Vael caravans. Hester may keep a duplicate section in a grain-measuring chest as a safeguard.
  • Optional complications: Corvin may have ordered an unauthorized attack; Branka may have released falsely indebted prisoners. Both remain possible future hooks rather than established events or explanations for the party's raid.

How Koss views the other houses

  • Vael: An enemy. Corvin's alleged interest in joining it creates a private complication; proof of Vael sponsoring the party is not established.
  • Morcant: Useful intelligence from people whose indirect methods differ from Koss's. Corvin is the named buyer.
  • Harrow: Irritating, socially unimpressive, and annoyingly unwilling to be provoked. Business remains possible; Corvin and Peregrine have a private connection.
  • Drazek: Barbarians lacking proper discipline. Previous fighting demonstrates the danger of pursuing them into their mountain terrain.
  • Belrath: A potential employer despite contempt between its scholars and Koss's soldiers. Mutual dislike does not rule out paid service.

How other houses view Koss

  • Vael: An existing enemy. The party attacked Koss-associated vampires without declaring Vael's sponsorship; Koss has not been shown to have proven that connection.
  • Morcant: Crude and messy, but usable. Corvin buys Morcant intelligence; Morcant can hire or steer Koss's force.
  • Harrow: Warriors deserving pity for their methods. Harrow's calm response to bullying frustrates them; necessary business and Peregrine's private connection remain possible.
  • Drazek: Rigid soldiers who subordinate morality to payment and rank. Previous fighting makes this a practical conflict as well as a philosophical one.
  • Belrath: Brutes it can afford to hire when useful. Contempt and paid cooperation can coexist.

Current adventure connection
