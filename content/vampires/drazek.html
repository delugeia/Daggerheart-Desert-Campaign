Character, holdings, and sigil

Drazek is a reclusive community of mountain warriors in the Pharaoh's Teeth. It protects ancient dangerous objects, particularly those whose power or possible possession makes circulation unsafe. Its members generally want custody so others cannot use the objects, rather than to profit from selling them.

They know secret passages, maintain remote way stations, and may charge for passage. Stealth, coordinated signals, trained animals, and terrain knowledge make their guerrilla defense formidable. They have fought Koss before and are exceptionally difficult to defeat at home. No precise supernatural combat ability follows from that reputation.

Houndwatch retains its sheltered escarpment, practical stores, kennels, and memorials to guides who brought travelers home. Its triangular sigil bears a crimson hound beneath three pale stars. The maxim is “Finish the pursuit.”

Drazek Members

Lord Othmar Drazek — head and guardian

Scarred, blunt, and patient in the field, Othmar values preparation and loyalty. He judges who deserves protection or punishment by a personal code, rather than accepting every paying employer's wishes. He respects Balthazar's knowledge and accepts information that helps his custodial duty, even while enduring criticism of his manners. He and Ysabet Morcant have a secret romance. His elder-god devotion adds another purpose to guardianship; the extent of each subordinate's knowledge remains unsettled.

Othmar is a muscular, weathered man appearing in his late fifties, with tan skin, a broad nose, and a healed scar running from his right brow toward his cheek. Gray-streaked hair is tied back above a rough beard; repaired leather armor, a dark cloak, and a hound clasp complete his hunter’s silhouette.

Dame Ruxandra Drazek — hunt captain

An exceptional tracker responsible for guarding approaches, recovering dangerous objects, and keeping expeditions alive. She questions Othmar's exceptions as a loyal counselor. She can argue for stronger protections or better evidence before a retrieval without plotting against him. Her competence gives her standing to disagree within an otherwise strongly loyal household.

Ruxandra is an athletic adult woman with olive skin, an angular face, and dark hair gathered into a thick braid. Patched leather armor, a fur-edged hunting coat, a net, and a sheathed blade suit her work; her direct gaze rarely yields to rank.

Gideon Flint — trail master

Gideon maintains shelters, water caches, equipment, and escort schedules. He is the practical reason many journeys succeed and refuses demands to move without adequate supplies. The guardians respect skill enough to let him direct travel decisions. His work exemplifies the household’s respect for competence and mutual obligation. He can negotiate with dwarf communities and travelers without turning every encounter into an invitation to the hidden holdings.

Gideon is a stocky man appearing in his fifties, with deep brown skin, a shaved head, and a salt-and-pepper beard. Faded ochre layers carry practical ropes, pouches, and water supplies. He stands with the easy balance of a guide accustomed to difficult ground.

Vera Drazek — outrider and the party's future contact

The youngest vampire in the depicted household is alert, earnest, and eager to prove useful. Her friendships with mortal guides fit genuine protective obligations. She belongs to the proposed intimate circle with Corvin Koss, Master Peregrine Harrow, and Faris Reed. Faris tells her Quill has hired the party to clear a dwarven temple. Vera will find the party on the way or during the expedition and ask that an important artifact be given to Drazek for safekeeping. Whether she knows a deeper purpose for that object is not established.

Vera is a young-looking adult woman with tan skin, short tousled dark hair, and an alert, earnest expression. A repaired rust-red cloak covers light leather gear; her compact traveling kit and ready stance distinguish her from the heavier-equipped senior hunters.

Apparent ages do not establish the vampires' chronological ages.

Historical background

During the Cistern Siege, Drazek guides evacuated households through passages others thought impassable. Its memorials celebrate such rescues. The Compact discourages assaults on dependents and protects routes, but Balthazar's archive does not command Drazek as a court.

Mountain dwarves offer blood in return for genuine protection, not extortion. Outsiders are usually turned away rather than killed on sight. Harrow's traveling network has more regular access than most houses, while Morcant knows the custodial secret and may entrust further objects to the guardians.

Attitude toward mortals

Drazek is proud, wary, and paternalistic. It respects demonstrated competence and honors protective obligations. Othmar compensates guides' dependents in keeping with that code. The household can dislike intrusive travelers while keeping faith with dwarf communities. Its self-image as a protector does not settle the morality of its secret religion or permit assumed slaughter of anyone who discovers a route.

Secrets, beliefs, and internal tensions

  • Elder-god purpose: Drazek secretly worships an ancient elder god and quietly waits for an unidentified artifact that can return that god to power. The deity and the consequences are not defined. A world-destruction plan is not established.
  • Romance: Othmar and Ysabet's private relationship deepens the Morcant connection.
  • Vera's circle: Her relationships cross institutional rivalries; they do not establish betrayal of Drazek or a common political conspiracy.
  • Separate open objects: The elder-god artifact, the proposed object reserved for a chosen person, and the expedition find Vera wants are not yet confirmed to be the same thing.
  • Open abilities: Wolves, foxes, and individually emerging animal forms were discussed. No fixed transformation rule is adopted.
  • Optional hooks: Vera's knowledge of an approach to a Koss storehouse can still help a captive rescue. A leaked route can involve stolen or misused Harrow credentials, but no current accusation against Isolde or rupture of the houses' mutual respect is established.

How Drazek views the other houses

  • Vael: Pretentious aristocrats. Vael's fear of Drazek's power need not produce open war.
  • Koss: Rigid soldiers who subordinate morality to payment and rank. Previous fighting makes this a practical conflict as well as a philosophical one.
  • Morcant: Respected keepers who understand the custodial duty and support it with information.
  • Harrow: Useful and generally acceptable, though excessively entangled with humans. Mutual respect and reliable trade outweigh social differences.
  • Belrath: A source of dangerous circulation: scholars and patrons who recover, sell, or publish things Drazek would contain. Vera and Faris nevertheless have a personal channel.

How other houses view Drazek

  • Vael: Uncouth and untamed, but powerful enough to fear.
  • Koss: Barbarians lacking proper discipline. Previous fighting demonstrates the danger of pursuing them into their mountain terrain.
  • Morcant: Respected custodians. Differences in presentation do not erase shared purpose or the option of handing over dangerous finds.
  • Harrow: Liked and respected for lacking aristocratic pretension. Harrow reaches them through travel and supplies; their suspicion of excessive mortal contact need not create hostility.
  • Belrath: Guardians who keep discoveries from circulation. Their competing views create the expedition dispute, complicated by Vera and Faris's private connection.

Current adventure connection

Do not substitute a Morcant messenger, assume the find powers the elder god, or decide in advance that Vera is lying about safekeeping.
