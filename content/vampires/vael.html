Character, holdings, and sigil

Vael cultivates aristocratic authority through titles, introductions, patronage, and carefully placed obligations. Seraphine leads a regional chapter of a wider vampire organization. Its name, hierarchy, and her exact superior remain undefined. Vael's vampire membership is selective: recognized rank matters, including prestigious religious or civic status. Mortal servants and lieutenants can be numerous without receiving the same invitation to immortality.

Discreet lending and luxury-caravan interests supply additional wealth. Vael's most important business is political influence. Its maxim is “Nothing freely given is without value.” Its circular sigil places a silver thorn around a crimson drop; the mark does not establish a magical surveillance power.

Ravenhollow Manor stands behind a low stone wall on a wooded, mist-shrouded ridge. Upland springs sustain its cool gardens amid the desert region. Shaded courts, deep cisterns, and doors reserved for particular guests make hospitality an expression of rank.

Vael Members

Lady Seraphine Vael — regional head

An elegant, calculating vampire who offers enough help to make accepting feel sensible. She values noble credentials, loyalty, and discretion, and prefers outsiders to conduct operations that could embarrass her household. She is the party's existing patron and Tamsin's lover. Her rivalry with Lenora Belrath is personal; it does not prevent useful contact between their houses. Her political polish conceals anxiety about appearances and the legitimacy of her own dependents.

Seraphine is a slender woman appearing in her early forties, with cool olive skin, an angular face, dark eyes, and black hair braided into a crown with silver accents. Charcoal and crimson layers, a silver thorn clasp, and measured, upright stillness convey controlled authority.

Edmund Graves — majordomo and intermediary

Edmund arranged the party's introduction and keeps the household functioning through impeccable manners and a formidable memory. Before becoming a vampire he had children; he has followed their mortal descendants through generations. Grim is one of those distant descendants. Edmund rescued him as an orphan on the streets and brought him into the household without revealing the relationship. His old friendship with Professor Ambrose Quill now offers the party further employment. His exact noble title remains open.

Edmund is tall and lean, appearing in his sixties, with swept-back gray hair, a neat mustache, and an attentive, lined face. His dark embroidered livery is carefully maintained; he stands straight with his hands folded, watching guests without seeming to stare.

Grimsby “Grim” Nackle (mortal) — groundskeeper and servant

Small, hunched, gravelly voiced, and accustomed to unpleasant errands, Grim knows passages, servants, and useful hiding places. He believes Edmund took him in out of unexpected kindness; he does not know they are related. His loyalty is sharpened by a fear of being discarded. He has a knack for obtaining receipts and helping frightened servants; no particular betrayal is established.

Grim is very short and deeply hunched, with a crooked nose, prominent ears, and a creased, weathered face. Almost bald, he has only scattered wisps of dry gray hair. Ragged brown leather and patched linen emphasize his compact, crouching silhouette.

Dame Tamsin Vael — negotiator and Seraphine's lover

Tamsin handles practical agreements and moves more comfortably among travelers than Seraphine does. Her title marks recognized standing, though whether she earned it before joining Vael is undecided. Ambitious and impatient, she favors tangible alliances and dislikes endless indirect maneuvering. Her intimacy with Seraphine gives these disagreements weight without establishing a secret plan to betray her.

Tamsin is an adult woman with copper-brown skin, short dark curls, and a level, assertive gaze. Burgundy travel robes, worn leather belts, and a bronze scroll tube give her the practical appearance of someone accustomed to negotiating on the road.

Apparent ages do not establish the vampires' chronological ages.

Historical background

In accounts of the Cistern Siege, Seraphine sheltered displaced households from fighting over water, creating enduring clients and debts. She helped negotiate the Compact, a voluntary inter-house agreement discouraging attacks on acknowledged dependents. Morcant preserves its records, but cannot enforce it as a sovereign court. Evidence and coalition-building determine its practical force.

This history explains Vael's reputation for assistance with conditions. It does not establish that every current rival was an enemy in that conflict, or that the party knows its details.

Attitude toward mortals

Mortals have valued roles, but are not treated as social equals. Household practice forbids unauthorized killing among dependents and compensates designated donors; indebted people may still have limited bargaining power. Vael's preference for turning titled individuals helps explain why Grim remains mortal. Seraphine is thought likely to know Edmund's connection to Grim, but her knowledge is not as firmly established as Edmund's.

Secrets and internal tensions

  • Illegitimate status: A member not pictured has a title that is false or overstated. The person is unnamed and is not identified as Edmund. Morcant's genealogies could expose the weakness.
  • Hidden kinship: Edmund knows Grim is his descendant; Grim does not.
  • Corvin's ambition: Corvin Koss is accused of spending his house's money to gain status and join Vael. Seraphine's response and the feasibility of changing sire-defined house affiliation are unresolved.
  • Optional evidence hook: A Koss Black Ledger could expose captive purchases and attacks on Vael caravans. A page naming a compromised Vael agent and Grim's receipt are optional leads for a future investigation.

How Vael views the other houses

  • Koss: An existing enemy. The party attacked Koss-associated vampires without declaring Vael's sponsorship; Koss has not been shown to have proven that connection.
  • Morcant: Friendly or tentatively allied. Seraphine values its knowledge while disliking its ability to puncture claims of importance.
  • Harrow: Common-born upstarts. Contact is principally political; Vael has less need of merchant services than some rivals do.
  • Drazek: Uncouth and untamed, but powerful enough to fear.
  • Belrath: The houses can cooperate. Seraphine and Lenora compete and dislike one another; Edmund and Quill work around that friction.

How other houses view Vael

  • Koss: An enemy. Corvin's alleged interest in joining it creates a private complication; proof of Vael sponsoring the party is not established.
  • Morcant: Broadly neutral and less impressed by aristocratic claims than Vael would like. Its records may contradict the house's preferred self-image.
  • Harrow: Crusty, blue-blooded aristocrats who look down on Harrow. Dealings are mostly political; Vael's demand for trading services is limited.
  • Drazek: Pretentious aristocrats. Vael's fear of Drazek's power need not produce open war.
  • Belrath: Institutionally compatible despite Lenora and Seraphine's personal rivalry. Quill and Edmund keep a useful channel open.

Unidentified connections

Additional possible relationships between unnamed Vael and Harrow members, and a suggested visitor to Grim, remain unidentified. Their connection to Corvin, Peregrine, Vera, and Faris is unknown.

Current adventure connection

Edmund thanks the party for its previous work and introduces Quill as a source of employment. This leads into Belrath's dwarven-ruin expedition. The assistance Seraphine originally promised remains unspecified.
